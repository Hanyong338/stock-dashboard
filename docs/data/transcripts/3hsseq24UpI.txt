In fact,  recent forecasts are gradually tilting  toward the Democratic Party,  represented by the color blue. If the  Democrats take both the House and the  Senate, it's called a "Blue Wave," so  if this becomes a reality, which  sectors might move? That would be  important. The hint lies in the market  immediately after the 2024 presidential  election. Immediately after President  Trump's election, the sectors that were  strong in the market were small and  mid-cap stocks like the Russell 2000,  as well as financial and energy stocks.  Expectations for tax cuts, deregulation , and expanded fossil fuel production  drove the so-called "Trump trade"  stocks to move strongly. Conversely,  the sectors that were weak were solar,  renewable energy, and electric vehicles . These are sectors that are far from  President Trump’s policy direction.  So, there is a view that this time, we  might see the opposite picture in the  market. That is the perspective  currently emerging. Hyundai Motor  Securities also pointed out that if a  Blue Wave becomes reality, there is a  possibility of a movement in the  opposite direction of the 2024 "Trump  trade" immediately after the election.  This means the sectors that were strong  back then may take a break, while the  sectors that were suppressed could gain  momentum. That is why, in Korea,  eco-friendly power infrastructure  sectors like solar, wind, and ESS are  being discussed as Blue Wave-related  sectors. However, if we look at the  2024 case from a longer perspective:  The strength of the policy-beneficiary  stocks did not last very long. They  surged briefly, rising for about a  month, but if you look at it from a  long-term perspective, the momentum  faded. While election results quickly  moved stock prices initially, over time , the market eventually starts looking  back at how actual policies unfold, how  interest rates move, and whether  corporate earnings follow.
