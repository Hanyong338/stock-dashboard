A CPU  is like one very smart university  professor. A GPU is like ten thousand  first-grade elementary school students.  Now, let's pose a question. Calculate 1  + 1 ten thousand times. Then, the CPU,  that very smart university professor,  will calculate 1 + 1 sequentially ten  thousand times. It takes a very long  time. On the other hand, since GPUs  have 10,000 elementary students  calculating 1 + 1 all at once, they  perform computations that used to take  a long time in parallel, which  significantly speeds up processing. So  in the case of a CPU, since one  university student handles the  processing, a single DRAM that can  store important information during the  process is sufficient. Conversely,  since GPUs require memory storage for  those 10,000 elementary students, they  have no choice but to stack DRAM  vertically.
